## Problem Title
SIH 26033: Multiple intermediaries reduce farmers earnings and increase consumer prices.

## Problem Creater's Organization
Ministry of Consumer Affairs, Food & Public Distribution

## Theme
Agriculture, FoodTech & Rural Development

## Problem Description
Expected Solution: Create a digital marketplace that:

• Connects farmers/FPOs directly with consumers and bulk buyers.
• Provides logistics support.
• Uses AI for demand forecasting and route optimization.

Benefits:

• Better prices for farmers.
• Lower prices for consumers.
• Reduced supply chain inefficiencies.

## Proposed Solution
1. Proposed Solution

We propose "Farm2Door AI", a smart digital marketplace that connects farmers directly with consumers, supermarkets, hotels, and wholesale buyers.

Farmers can upload their products, quantity, and prices using a mobile application. Customers can compare prices and buy fresh agricultural products directly from farmers.

The platform uses Artificial Intelligence (AI) to predict demand, suggest fair prices, and find the best delivery routes. It also connects nearby farmers to share transportation and reduce delivery costs.

 2. How It Addresses the Problem

Reduces middlemen:Farmers sell directly to buyers.
Better prices: Farmers receive a larger share of the selling price.
Lower costs:Customers can buy products at competitive prices.
 Easy transportation: Nearby farmers can share delivery vehicles.
 Less food waste: AI predicts demand and helps farmers plan their sales.
Price transparency: Buyers can compare prices before purchasing.

3. Innovation and Uniqueness

Our solution includes three unique features:

1. Group Delivery: Nearby farmers combine their products and share transportation costs.
2. Smart Price Predictor: AI analyses demand, supply, and market trends to suggest suitable prices.
3. Farm Fresh Tracking: Customers can check the farmer's details, product origin, and delivery status.

The application will support local languages to make it easy for farmers to use.

Our goal is to increase farmers' income, reduce consumer prices, and create a more efficient agricultural supply chain.

## Feasibility and Viability

1. Analysis of the Feasibility of the Idea
High operational feasibility due to rising smartphone penetration and active government backing of digital infrastructure for FPOs (e.g., e-NAM initiatives).

Scalable architecture leveraging existing third-party logistics (3PL) providers alongside local transport networks to minimize initial capital expenditure on fleet management.

2. Potential Challenges and Risks
Low digital literacy among smallholder farmers.

Resistance from established traditional intermediaries and local mandis.

Perishable nature of goods causing high sensitivity to transport delays.

3. Strategies for Overcoming These Challenges
Digital Literacy: Introduce vernacular, voice-assisted interfaces and partner with local FPO leaders to assist farmers with onboarding and listings.

Intermediary Transition: Incentivize progressive commission agents to transition into licensed logistics or aggregation partners on the platform.

Cold-Chain Integration: Partner with cold-storage operators and integrate real-time temperature tracking for perishable produce.
